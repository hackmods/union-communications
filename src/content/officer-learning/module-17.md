# Module 17: Position Description Forms & Reclassification

## How to use this module

Allow **20–30 minutes**. Bring the member's **current Position Description Form (PDF)**, any **draft update** the manager just issued, the **pay-band / job-evaluation article** in your collective agreement, and a notepad. Read in a private space — duty logs record who directed the work and can name colleagues.

Sections move from PDF anatomy through duty dumps, a 14-day frequency log, Employee Comments, and Joint Job Evaluation Committee (JJEC) or classification-grievance escalation. Pair with **Module 1** before you file. **This module** owns steward discipline for **auditing PDFs**, logging **actual duties**, writing **Employee Comments**, and starting a **reclassification** file. **Module 16** owns seniority **lists** and bumping trees — not pay-band job evaluation. **Module 10** owns joint committees generally; use it when the forum is a JHSC or LMC, not a job-eval panel. **Module 8** owns settlement wording once the file is filed.

**CLC** steward education, **CUPE** classification materials, and **Unifor** job-evaluation training deepen factor reading. Review windows and JJEC existence are **CA-specific** — confirm yours before you quote a number of days.

🪞 Reflection: A manager's "routine cleanup" of a PDF is not a courtesy. It is a claim about the job. Your job is to test that claim against the work actually done.

## Overarching Purpose
To equip Ontario stewards with the evidence discipline to audit Position Description Forms, document uncompensated scope creep, write Employee Comments inside the CA review window, and initiate a classification grievance or joint job-evaluation review without treating one sector's point-factor grid as universal law.

## Core Learning Objectives
*   **Know**: That a PDF is the employer's official description of regular, recurring duties and the usual foundation for pay-band classification; how point-factor plans typically score education, complexity, accountability, communication, physical or sensory demand, and working conditions (labels vary by plan); and that review windows and JJEC processes come from **your CA**, not from this module.
*   **Feel/Understand**: Confident challenging uncompensated duty dumps; protective of bargaining-unit work boundaries; unwilling to let "administrative cleanup" become a silent pay cut.
*   **Be Able To**: Run an old-vs-new PDF audit; help a member complete a 10–14 working-day Duty Frequency Log; draft Employee Comments and sign **Received & Contested**; and start Module 1 intake for a classification grievance or JJEC referral.

## 1. Anatomy of a Position Description Form (PDF)
A Position Description Form is not a job posting. It is the employer's written claim about **regular, recurring** work assigned to a position — usually the document a point-factor or JJEC plan rates when it places the job on a pay grid.

| Piece | What it usually means | Steward check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Regular / recurring duties** | Daily, weekly, or monthly core work, often with a **percentage of time** | Do the percentages match a two-week log, or only the manager's wish list? |
| **Occasional / periodic duties** | Seasonal, peak, or coverage tasks | Is "occasional" doing 40% of the week? That is regular work wearing a soft label. |
| **Qualifications** | Education, experience, licences the position requires | Added credentials after hire can be a dump — compare to the last signed PDF. |
| **Independence / decision-making** | Follows procedures vs. modifies them and resolves exceptions | A one-line upgrade here often moves complexity and accountability factors. |
| **Physical / sensory / environment** | Lifting, standing, screen focus, hazards | Unrecorded load increases belong in comments even when pay is the main fight. |

💡 Note: Some workplaces call this a job fact sheet, job description, or classification questionnaire. The **job** is the same: a dated employer description you can audit. Use the name in **your CA**.

⚠️ Warning: Signing a draft PDF without comments can be treated as agreement that the description — and the unchanged pay band — is accurate. **Consequence:** the union later argues against the member's own signature. **Remedy:** review with a steward inside the CA window; attach written Employee Comments; sign **Received & Contested**; keep a copy.

## 2. Spotting Duty Dumps and Scope Creep
Managers often present an updated PDF as "housekeeping." Train members to read for **higher-rated work at the same rate of pay**.

| Red flag | What it looks like on the page | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Supervisory / lead work** | "Assign, check, and guide junior staff" added to a non-lead classification | Lead or supervisory factors usually sit on a higher band — **check your plan**. |
| **Elevated independence** | "Follows procedures" becomes "modifies procedures and resolves complex exceptions" | Complexity and accountability points move even if the title stays put. |
| **System administration** | Database, access, or architecture duties added to clerical or support work | Tool *use* is not the same as system *ownership*. Log the difference. |
| **Hidden physical / sensory load** | Continuous monitoring, heavier lifts, or new hazards with no environment update | Working-conditions factors are easy to skip when the fight is about software. |

Duty dumps also steal **bargaining-unit work** from higher-rated classifications. If a Band 8 is doing Band 10 work unpaid, the Band 10 complement shrinks.

**CUPE** classification stewards and **Unifor** job-eval materials both treat the PDF as evidence, not courtesy stationery.

⚠️ Warning: "Everyone is doing more since the vacancy" is not a defence. **Consequence:** unpaid higher-level work becomes the new normal. **Remedy:** log frequency and who directed the work; file toward reclassification or a complement grievance as **your CA** allows.

## 3. The 14-Day Duty Frequency Audit Log
When the member disputes the draft, gather **objective time evidence** before the review window closes.

### What to log (10–14 consecutive working days)
*   Task name in the member's words, then mapped to a PDF line.
*   Minutes or hours spent that day.
*   Frequency label: daily, weekly, monthly — and whether that label is honest.
*   Software or tools, and the **level of guidance** required (procedure vs. exception).
*   Who assigned or signed off on the work.

### Comparison sheet
Lay the log beside the **old PDF** and the **draft PDF**. Highlight:
1.  Duties present in the log and missing from the draft.
2.  Duties the draft calls occasional that the log shows as regular.
3.  New higher-level lines with no pay-band movement.
4.  Time percentages that cannot add to 100% without fiction.

📝 Practice: Pick one week from a real (or redacted) schedule. Write five lines: task, minutes, frequency, tool, who directed. Then write one sentence: "This is regular work because…"

💡 Note: Fourteen days is a teaching default because it captures a full pay period in many workplaces. If your CA or JJEC package asks for a different window, **use that window**.

## 4. PDF Dispute and Reclassification Procedure
Clocks and forums are **CA-specific**. The sequence below is the usual steward path — confirm articles before you quote days.

1.  **Receipt of draft PDF** — date-stamp when the member received it. The review clock usually starts here, not when the steward first sees it.
2.  **Review window** — many agreements give about **10 to 15 working days** to review with a steward. That is a common range, not Ontario law. Calendar **your** article on day one.
3.  **Employee Comments** — if the description is wrong, **do not leave the comments box blank**. Attach a dated letter that lists missing duties, wrong percentages, and higher-level work. Sign the form **Received & Contested**. Keep a copy; ask the employer to acknowledge receipt.
4.  **Escalation** — if management refuses to amend the PDF or move the pay band, the union files a **classification grievance** and/or refers the file to the **Joint Job Evaluation Committee (JJEC)** where the CA creates one.

**Module 1** owns the 6 W's and FAR before the first step meeting. **Module 8** owns open-door wording and four-corners settlements after you file. **Module 10** is the wrong primary map if the only forum is a job-eval committee.

⚠️ Warning: Silence inside the review window can be treated as acceptance. **Consequence:** the new PDF locks and the pay band stays. **Remedy:** written comments before the CA deadline; preserve the draft and the log; escalate on paper.

## 5. Point-Factor Job Evaluation (Labels Vary)
Most public-sector and college support plans score a **position**, not how hard the incumbent "tries." Factor names differ (Hay, JJEC, college grids). Teach the **clusters**, then open **your** rating manual.

| Common cluster | What raters usually look for |
| --- | --- |
| **Education & experience** | Formal credentials and time-in-role the **position** requires |
| **Complexity & judgement** | Problem difficulty; how often exceptions appear |
| **Accountability & independence** | Consequence of error; freedom to change the method |
| **Communication & interpersonal** | Who must be influenced, and in what setting |
| **Physical / sensory demand** | Effort, concentration, and duration |
| **Working conditions** | Hazards, interruptions, unpleasant environment |

A duty dump that adds independence or system ownership is a **factor** argument, not a personality argument. Bring the log and the old-vs-new audit; do not argue "they work hard."

💡 Note: External non-union salary surveys do not replace your CA grid. If the employer cites market, ask where the CA lets market override the plan.

## 6. Worked practice — three rounds

**Round 1 — Old vs. new audit.** Take a two-page redacted pair (or write six lines of "old" and six of "new"). Mark every added supervisory, independence, or system-admin phrase. Write the pay band shown on each page. If the band did not move, that is your first question — not your last.

**Round 2 — Duty log excerpt.** From a 14-day log, total minutes on "guide new staff" and "reset user access." Convert to a percentage of paid hours. Compare to the draft's "occasional" label.

**Round 3 — Employee Comments draft.** Write a half-page letter: date received, CA review article, three contested lines, two log facts, the ask (amend PDF and/or refer to JJEC / classification grievance). Sign Received & Contested.

🪞 Reflection: Which PDF in your local was signed "to get them off my back" — and what work is now unpaid?

## Common pitfalls

1.  **Housekeeping myth** — Treating a rewritten PDF as clerical when factors moved.
2.  **Blank comments** — Signing without a contest letter so the employer records agreement.
3.  **No log** — Arguing "they do more" without dated minutes and who directed the work.
4.  **Invented clocks** — Quoting 10 or 15 days as law when the CA says something else.
5.  **One-grid worship** — Applying another sector's point-factor table as Ontario-wide truth.
6.  **Seniority mix-up** — Using Module 16 bumping logic to decide a pay band.
7.  **Verbal deals** — Accepting "we'll look at it next year" without a dated referral.

## Self-Test Quiz

### Question 1
Management hands a member an updated Position Description Form (PDF) that adds system administration duties and guiding new staff, but leaves their pay rate unchanged. What should the steward advise the member to do first?
*   A) Refuse to read the document and immediately walk out of the supervisor's office.
*   B) Sign the PDF immediately without reading it, because manager signatures are final.
*   C) Review the draft with the steward, log actual daily tasks, and prepare written Employee Comments highlighting the added higher-level duties.
*   D) File a health and safety refusal because system administration causes eye strain.

**Correct Answer: C**
*Explanation*: Members should never blindly sign a contested PDF nor flatly refuse to review it. The proper first step is to review the draft with a steward, document actual time on higher-level tasks, and attach detailed written Employee Comments before escalating to a reclassification grievance or JJEC.

### Question 2
In point-factor job evaluation systems, how are job pay bands determined?
*   A) By the supervisor's personal opinion of how hard the worker tries.
*   B) By scoring core factors such as Education, Complexity/Judgement, Accountability, Physical/Sensory Effort, and Working Conditions.
*   C) By counting the exact number of words in the job description title.
*   D) By matching the worker's salary to external non-union corporate benchmarks.

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation*: Point-factor plans rate the **position** by assigning points across core factor clusters. Labels vary by plan. They do not rate how hard the incumbent tries, and market surveys do not replace your CA grid unless the agreement says so.

### Question 3
What is the primary purpose of a 14-Day Duty Frequency Audit Log?
*   A) To spy on co-workers' lunch break habits.
*   B) To provide objective, time-stamped evidence of actual daily tasks and duration to prove management's PDF draft is inaccurate.
*   C) To replace the collective agreement during wage negotiations.
*   D) To automatically calculate overtime pay rates without manager approval.

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation*: A Duty Frequency Audit Log tracks exact time on tasks over about two working weeks so the union can prove higher-level duties are regular and recurring, not minor occasional extras.

### Question 4
The member disagrees with several duty percentages on the draft PDF. The comments box is empty and the manager wants a signature today. Best steward advice?
*   A) Leave comments blank and sign so the file can "move along."
*   B) Write detailed Employee Comments, sign Received & Contested, and keep a copy.
*   C) Refuse all signatures forever and wait for arbitration to describe the job.
*   D) Tell the member to resign and apply to the higher-rated posting instead.

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation*: Blank comments read as agreement. The member records the contest on the form, keeps a copy, and preserves the right to escalate inside the CA process.

### Question 5
A draft PDF lists "reset user access and train new clerks" as occasional. The 14-day log shows those tasks took 38% of paid hours. Strongest union angle?
*   A) Occasional always means whatever the manager types.
*   B) Treat the log as proof the work is regular and recurring, and contest the percentage and factor rating.
*   C) Ignore percentages; only the job title controls the pay band.
*   D) File a Module 16 bumping grievance because 38% is a seniority issue.

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation*: Regular vs. occasional is an evidence question. Time logs support a factor and percentage challenge. Bumping (Module 16) is a different file about displacement order, not pay-band rating.

### Question 6
Your CA gives 12 working days to review a draft PDF with the union. HR says "sign by Friday" three days after delivery. What should the steward do first?
*   A) Accept Friday because verbal HR deadlines always control.
*   B) Calendar the CA window from the date of receipt, file comments inside that window, and challenge any shortened deadline in writing.
*   C) Wait until day 20 so the log looks more complete, even if the CA window has closed.
*   D) Quote "15 working days" as Ontario law and ignore the CA article.

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation*: Review windows come from your CA. Stewards calendar the contractual clock on day one and contest employer shortcuts in writing. Do not invent a statutory number of days, and do not miss the real window to finish a perfect log.
