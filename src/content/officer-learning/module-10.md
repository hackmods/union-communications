# Module 10: Coordinating Local & Joint Workplace Committees

## How to use this module

Allow **40–50 minutes**. Bring your JHSC terms of reference (or OHSA-required structure notes), any Labour-Management Committee (LMC) minutes, and a current hazard or workload complaint from the floor. Sketch who sits where using `/tools/org-chart` before the practice drill — committee power is useless if stewards cannot find the right door.

Sections move from JHSC vs. LMC mandates through United Caucus Discipline, multi-committee escalation, and OHSA work-refusal routes that engage the JHSC. Pair with `/guide/joint-committee` and `/guide/right-to-refuse`. **CLC** health and safety education and provincial OHSA concepts deepen statutory powers; LMC practice stays consultative unless your CA says otherwise.

🪞 Reflection: Joint tables are caucuses with a purpose — not audiences for management presentations.

## Overarching Purpose
To train local leaders to use Joint Health and Safety Committees and Labour-Management Committees as coordinated power centres — with united caucus discipline, clear statutory vs. consultative boundaries, and multi-door escalation that moves hazards, workload, and contract friction together instead of trapping issues in the wrong room.

## Core Learning Objectives
*   **Know**: The legal mandates, powers, and limits of the Joint Health and Safety Committee (JHSC) and the Labour-Management Committee (LMC); the rule of "United Caucus Discipline".
*   **Feel/Understand**: That joint committees are powerful strategic caucuses to build collective bargaining strength, rather than administrative management-led talk shops.
*   **Be Able To**: Route member issues to the most effective forum (JHSC, LMC, or Grievance); prepare joint committee briefing packages; enforce safety-based work refusals.

---

## 1. JHSC vs. LMC: Powers and Limits
Do not treat every joint meeting as the same meeting.

| Dimension | JHSC | LMC |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legal source | Workplace safety legislation (**OHSA** concepts in Ontario) | Collective agreement / practice — usually consultative |
| Core focus | Hazards, violence, air quality, ergonomics, critical injuries | Workload, schedules, operational policy, contract friction |
| Inspection / investigation | Statutory inspection and critical-injury investigation roles | No automatic statutory inspect power |
| Recommendations | Formal recommendations; employer typically must answer **in writing within a legislative timeline** (often cited as 21 days) | Discussion and recommendations without the same statutory force |
| Best use | Force documented safety responses | Surface systemic ops issues before mass grievances |

`/guide/joint-committee` details how locals keep joint bodies from becoming management lecture halls. Use `/tools/org-chart` so members know which reps own which table.

💡 Note: Putting a pure scheduling gripe only on the JHSC agenda wastes statutory time; putting a critical hazard only on the LMC agenda wastes legal power.

---

## 2. United Caucus Discipline
Before any joint meeting, union reps hold a **private pre-meeting (caucus)**:

*   Align on facts, asks, and walk-away lines.
*   Assign speaking roles (lead, evidence, note-taker).
*   Decide which items escalate to grievance if the employer stalls.
*   Resolve internal disagreements **before** management enters the room.

**United Caucus Discipline:** Never disagree, argue, or break ranks in front of management. A public split teaches the employer which rep to peel off. Debate hard in caucus; speak with one voice at the table.

⚠️ Warning: "Just being honest" in front of HR is not transparency — it is free intelligence for the other side.

---

## 3. Multi-Committee Escalation
Serious issues rarely fit one door. Example: understaffing that causes injuries and contract violations.

Effective escalation runs **in parallel**:

1.  **JHSC** — hazard inspection, recommendations, written employer response clock.
2.  **LMC** — workload, scheduling policy, operational fixes on the record.
3.  **Grievance / group grievance** — CA staffing, health and safety, or workload clauses.
4.  **Bargaining file** — pattern evidence for the next round.

Briefing packages should travel with the issue: chronology, photos, incident reports, prior minutes, and the specific ask for each forum. **CLC** health and safety courses stress paper trails — verbal "we'll look into it" dies at the next shift change.

📝 Practice: Take one live floor issue and write a three-line routing plan: JHSC ask / LMC ask / grievance theory.

---

## 4. Work Refusals & the JHSC Route
When a worker refuses unsafe work under **OHSA** concepts, the steward's job is process discipline — not heroics. Route refusals through the statutory steps and engage **JHSC** certified members / committee processes as the law and your workplace procedure require. Pair floor coaching with `/guide/right-to-refuse`.

Steward checklist mindset:

*   Member reports the hazard and refuses unsafe work in good faith.
*   Supervisor investigates; union JHSC representation is involved as required.
*   If unresolved, escalate through the OHSA refusal path (including ministry involvement where triggered).
*   Parallel: get the hazard onto the JHSC formal agenda so recommendations and written responses follow even after the immediate refusal ends.

Do not trade a refusal for an LMC "discussion next month" while the hazard remains live.

💡 Note: A work refusal protects the moment; JHSC recommendations protect the system. Use both.

## Worked scenario

**The understaffed line.** A department runs short every afternoon. Sprains climb; members report rushing on unsafe guards. One steward wants only a grievance. Another wants only an LMC chat. A third argues with the first in front of HR about whether a guard is "really necessary."

**Apply:** Call caucus — enforce United Caucus Discipline (Section 2). Route injuries/guards to JHSC inspection and recommendations; put workload/scheduling on LMC; file a group grievance on contract and safety-related clauses (Section 3). Present one united ask set in each room.

**Don't apply:** Do not air the guard debate in front of management. Do not strand the hazard on LMC alone. Do not wait for arbitration before using JHSC statutory recommendation power.

**Second beat — refusal.** A member refuses to operate a machine with a disabled interlock. A manager suggests "bring it up at the next LMC."

**Apply:** Support the OHSA refusal path and JHSC involvement now (`/guide/right-to-refuse`). Log the hazard for formal JHSC recommendation. Use LMC later for staffing policy that caused bypass pressure — not as a substitute for the refusal.

**Don't apply:** Do not send the member back to the hazard pending a future committee date.

## Practice drill

Practice: **Round 1 — Caucus.** Two stewards disagree on whether understaffing is "safety" or "operations." Run a five-minute caucus to produce one united position and role assignments before a mock JHSC.

**Round 2 — Routing matrix.** For heat stress + forced overtime, fill: JHSC products, LMC products, grievance products, evidence list. Debrief: Did anyone break caucus discipline in the mock joint meeting?

🪞 Reflection: Name one issue your local keeps dumping in the wrong committee — and the door it should also open.

## Floor checklist

- [ ] Know which issues are JHSC (hazards) vs. LMC (ops/workload) vs. both
- [ ] Hold union caucus before every joint meeting
- [ ] Assign lead speaker, evidence lead, and note-taker in caucus
- [ ] Resolve internal disagreements privately — United Caucus Discipline
- [ ] Bring briefing packages: chronology, incidents, prior minutes, clear asks
- [ ] Use JHSC recommendation power to start the employer's written-response clock
- [ ] Put systemic workload/schedule friction on the LMC agenda on the record
- [ ] File grievances in parallel when CA rights are engaged
- [ ] Route OHSA work refusals through statutory steps — not "next month's LMC"
- [ ] Engage JHSC processes during and after refusals (`/guide/right-to-refuse`)
- [ ] Update `/tools/org-chart` so members know committee contacts
- [ ] Track employer written responses to JHSC recommendations to deadline
- [ ] Never trade a live hazard for vague future discussion
- [ ] Carry pattern evidence into bargaining when committees stall

## Common pitfalls

1. **Wrong door only** — Hazards stuck in LMC; workload stuck only in JHSC.
2. **Broken caucus** — Union reps arguing in front of management.
3. **Minutes as therapy** — Talking without asks, deadlines, or written recommendations.
4. **Refusal deferral** — Sending unsafe work to a future agenda instead of OHSA process.
5. **Single-track faith** — Waiting on one grievance while statutory JHSC power sits unused.

## Self-Test Quiz

### Question 1
During a Joint Health and Safety Committee (JHSC) meeting, a union rep and another union member start arguing in front of the HR manager about whether a safety guard is necessary. Which core rule of committee organizing was violated?
*   A) The rule of statutory consensus.
*   B) United Caucus Discipline.
*   C) The employer-disclosure standard.
*   D) The open-door grievance wording rule.

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation*: Union representatives must always present a completely united front to management during joint meetings. Any disagreements must be worked out in private pre-meetings (caucuses).

### Question 2
Which committee has the statutory, legally-mandated power to force an employer to respond to written safety recommendations within a set, legislative timeline?
*   A) The Labour-Management Committee (LMC).
*   B) The Joint Health and Safety Committee (JHSC).
*   C) The Executive Board Elections Committee.
*   D) The social committee planning the staff picnic.

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation*: Under health and safety legislation, the JHSC has unique statutory powers to make formal recommendations, and employers are legally required to provide a written response within a strict deadline (often 21 days).

### Question 3
A steward discovers that a department is facing severe stress and physical injuries due to systemic understaffing. Which is the most effective multi-committee escalation strategy?
*   A) File a single individual grievance and wait for arbitration.
*   B) Ignore the issue since understaffing is a management right.
*   C) Route the physical injuries to the JHSC for a hazard investigation, put the workload and scheduling friction on the LMC agenda, and file a group grievance for contract violations simultaneously.
*   D) Raise it only as a personal complaint to one friendly supervisor.

**Correct Answer: C**
*Explanation*: Escalating the issue through multiple joint committees and the grievance process simultaneously maximizes organizational pressure on the employer, forcing them to address the systemic understaffing early.

### Question 4
A worker refuses to operate equipment with a disabled safety interlock. Management says "put it on next month's LMC agenda." Best steward response?
*   A) Agree — LMC is the only lawful forum for refusals.
*   B) Support the OHSA work-refusal process now and engage JHSC pathways; use LMC later for related staffing policy if needed.
*   C) Order all workers to walk out immediately without process.
*   D) Tell the member to work carefully until the picnic committee meets.

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation*: Live refusals follow OHSA process and JHSC involvement; LMC discussion is not a substitute for removing the hazard.

### Question 5
What belongs in a union caucus that does **not** belong in the joint meeting with management present?
*   A) The final united ask the union will present.
*   B) Internal debate over tactics, disagreements, and which rep will lead — resolved before entering.
*   C) The written JHSC recommendation already served on the employer.
*   D) The attendance sheet for statutory quorum.

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation*: Caucus is where disagreement lives. The joint table hears one disciplined union position.

### Question 6
Which statement best captures the LMC's usual limit compared to the JHSC?
*   A) LMC can issue OHSA-style orders with ministry force by default.
*   B) LMC replaces the need for grievances whenever minutes exist.
*   C) LMC is typically consultative on operations and workload — useful for pressure and record-building, but without JHSC's statutory recommendation-response power.
*   D) LMC may never discuss scheduling or workload under any CA.

**Correct Answer: C**
*Explanation*: Know the divider: JHSC carries statutory safety teeth; LMC carries operational dialogue unless your agreement adds stronger bite.
