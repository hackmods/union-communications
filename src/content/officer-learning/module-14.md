# Module 14: Everyday Union Value: Orientation, Affinity & Community

## How to use this module

Allow **35–45 minutes**. Bring a draft welcome-kit checklist, any affinity discount agreements your local already has, and one community partner your executive could realistically stand beside this year. Read Module 6 only to **contrast** — Module 6 covers equity barriers, ADR, and bargaining equity clauses; this module covers Day-1 welcome culture, affinity cards without sharing lists, and community coalitions.

Sections move from the 15-minute orientation through secure affinity programs and community unionism. Pair with `/guide/membership-signup` for card signing and Module 11 for list-privacy rules. **CLC** member engagement education and **CUPE** steward welcome materials reinforce first-day contact; **Unifor** community campaigns show coalition models across Ontario.

🪞 Reflection: Members who only meet the union during a crisis think we are an insurance company. Day-1 welcome turns the union into their team.

## Overarching Purpose
To equip local executives and shop stewards with the tools to build everyday union pride, solidarity, and loyalty through brief Day-1 new member orientations, secure affinity discount programs, and community-based alliances — without sharing membership lists with vendors or confusing welcome culture with bargaining-table equity work.

## Core Learning Objectives
*   **Know**: The design of a 15-minute Day-1 orientation and welcome kit; the affinity model that verifies eligibility without releasing member databases; how community unionism links workplace struggles to public good.
*   **Feel/Understand**: Welcoming and supportive of brand-new workers; proud of the non-crisis value the union provides outside of grievances.
*   **Be Able To**: Run an effective 15-minute new member orientation; distribute union discount cards without sharing lists; organize community solidarity coalitions that strengthen strike readiness.

## 1. The 15-Minute Day-1 New Member Orientation
If a worker's first union contact is a discipline meeting or a strike vote, they will see the union as distant crisis management. **Day-1 welcome** sets the tone: supportive, practical, and proud.

### Negotiate the time block
Push for a collective agreement clause granting the union **15–30 minutes** during the employer's official onboarding — paid, private space, no manager present. Without the clause, stewards still greet new hires at break and schedule a huddle within the first week.

### The welcome kit (physical or digital folder)

| Item | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Welcome letter from the Local President | Human face of the local |
| Current collective agreement (or pocket summary + QR) | Wages, rights, safety |
| Steward name, photo, contact card | Someone to call before HR |
| Union membership card + pen | Sign on the spot — see `/guide/membership-signup` |
| Affinity discount card (if your local has one) | Everyday value before first dues |
| Calendar of next GMM or social event | Invitation, not obligation |

### The 15-minute script (keep it simple)
1.  **Welcome (2 min):** "We're glad you're here. The union is every member — not a downtown office."
2.  **Contract basics (5 min):** Wages, probation, safety, how to reach your steward.
3.  **Sign the card (5 min):** Voting rights, meeting notices, democratic voice — walk through `/guide/membership-signup`.
4.  **Questions + perks (3 min):** Affinity discounts, committee invites, no grievance lecture yet.

Do **not** spend fifteen minutes on arbitration timelines on someone's first morning.

💡 Note: A signed card on Day 1 beats chasing the same worker for three years during a ratification vote.

## 2. Affinity Benefits Without Sharing Lists
Affinity programs negotiate **exclusive discounts** — workwear, retail, auto insurance — using collective purchasing power. They build everyday loyalty when members feel the union in their wallet, not only at the bargaining table.

### The secure distribution model

| Step | Do | Don't |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verify eligibility | Issue a **union-branded discount card** or single-use code members present at checkout | Email the vendor a CSV of names and personal emails |
| Promote the perk | Newsletter blurb, orientation kit, poster in lunchroom | Let the vendor spam members directly |
| Renew annually | Reconfirm partnership terms; no data sharing clause in writing | Hand membership lists to "prove" headcount |
| Track uptake | Anonymous counts ("cards distributed") | Give vendors access to your database |

Module 11's **Membership List Directive** applies: sharing lists with commercial partners is a constitutional offense. A 15% boot discount is not worth selling member privacy.

**Unifor** and national union affinity programs often ship card-stock and rules — locals add shop-specific partners only with a **no-list-sharing** memo signed by the President.

⚠️ Warning: If a vendor demands your membership list to "activate" the discount, the answer is no — offer cards, not data.

📝 Practice: Draft a one-paragraph vendor agreement clause: "The local will not transfer member personal information; eligibility is verified by presentation of the official union discount card."

## 3. Community Unionism & Coalition Building
Workplace struggles do not exist in a vacuum. **Community unionism** connects bargaining goals to the health of the surrounding community — so when picket lines go up, neighbours already know why.

### Shared struggles (examples across Ontario sectors)
*   **Hospital staff:** Staffing ratios are patient wait times — partner with patient-advocacy and health coalitions.
*   **School support workers:** Classroom safety is children's safety — ally with parent councils and education coalitions.
*   **Municipal workers:** Public services are the social fabric — coordinate with transit riders and tenant unions.

### Coalition tactics that work
1.  **Show up first:** Sponsor food drives, park clean-ups, youth sports — logo visible, strings attached to no endorsement deal.
2.  **Joint press:** Community partners amplify bargaining asks that benefit the public, not only the table.
3.  **Strike solidarity bank:** Relationships built at the food bank pay back on the picket line.
4.  **Clear boundaries:** Support community campaigns without releasing member lists to partner NGOs — same rule as vendors.

**OFL** and local labour councils convene coalitions; **CLC** community-unionism education frames the long game.

🪞 Reflection: Name one public good your last bargaining demand protected — could a community partner have said it with you at a mic?

## 4. How This Differs from Module 6 (Building Collective Power)
Module 6 and Module 14 both strengthen solidarity — they are not the same course.

| Topic | Module 6 — Building Collective Power | Module 14 — Everyday Union Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Focus | Equity barriers, ADR, bargaining equity clauses | Day-1 welcome, affinity perks, community coalitions |
| Primary tools | Human rights routes, accommodation, CA language | Orientation kits, discount cards, public partnerships |
| List privacy | Protects marginalized members in complaints | Protects **all** members from vendor/political list sharing |
| Time horizon | Structural fixes across bargaining cycles | First-shift impression + year-round visible perks |
| Success metric | Enforceable equity wins in the CA | Signed cards, discount uptake, coalition allies at the line |

Stewards need both: Module 6 removes barriers; Module 14 makes the union feel worth joining on hour one.

💡 Note: Community coalitions are not a substitute for equity clauses — they amplify stories Module 6 wins at the table.

## Worked scenario

**Three new hires, one steward.** Onboarding day, HR gives you eighteen minutes in a break room. A workwear shop wants member emails for a 15% boot discount. The local also represents school custodians negotiating staffing language.

**Apply:** Run the 15-minute script (Section 1): welcome, contract basics, sign cards via `/guide/membership-signup`, hand affinity cards without collecting emails for the vendor (Section 2). Decline the CSV request; offer branded cards instead. Ask the custodian steward to invite a parent-council ally to the next staffing press event (Section 3) — community face on a workplace demand.

**Don't apply:** Do not lecture on grievance steps A–Z. Do not email the vendor your membership list. Do not confuse this orientation with Module 6 ADR training — save equity accommodation depth for case work.

**Second beat — ratification six months later.** Two of the three signed on Day 1; one did not. A contract vote is scheduled.

**Apply:** The two are eligible; target the third with a quick sign-up huddle — same welcome kit, five minutes, no shame. Affinity card still works for all dues payers; voting rights need the card.

**Don't apply:** Do not assume dues deduction equals a vote. Do not give the vendor the third worker's email "so they feel included."

## Practice drill

Practice: **Round 1 — Orientation script.** Time yourself delivering the four-beat script to a colleague in fifteen minutes. Check: Did you mention the steward before the arbitration clause?

**Round 2 — Vendor boundary.** Role-play the Treasurer refusing a list request. Offer cards, written no-sharing clause, and a union-branded poster for the store window.

**Round 3 — Coalition map.** Pick one local demand and name two community groups that would benefit from winning it. Draft three talking points they could repeat publicly.

🪞 Reflection: What did your union do for you before you needed a grievance?

## Common pitfalls

1. **Crisis-first introduction** — Workers meet the union only when disciplined or during a strike vote.
2. **Grievance lecture on Day 1** — Overwhelming new hires who still need a map to the lunchroom.
3. **List for discounts** — Trading member data for a retail percentage.
4. **Vendor spam** — Letting partners email members because "it's a good deal."
5. **Coalition cosplay** — Logo on a flyer without showing up for partners' fights.
6. **Confusing modules** — Teaching ADR equity frames when stewards still lack a welcome kit.

## Self-Test Quiz

### Question 1
A steward is running their first 15-minute Day-1 New Member Orientation session for three newly hired employees. Which of the following approaches is most effective for building long-term union engagement?
*   A) Spend the entire 15 minutes walking them through the strict legal steps of the formal grievance procedure.
*   B) Welcome them warmly, hand them a professional welcome folder with their collective agreement and steward contact card, explain that their contract guarantees their safety, and have them sign their union membership cards.
*   C) Give them a quick 2-minute greeting and tell them to look up the union's website if they ever get disciplined by management.
*   D) Delay orientation until the first contract campaign so the urgency motivates sign-ups.

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation*: First impressions matter. A Day-1 orientation should be welcoming, informative, and focused on building connection and security, ending with the crucial administrative step of signing their membership card.

### Question 2
A local workwear store offers to provide all members of your local with a 15% discount on work boots, but they demand that the Local Treasurer email them a list of all member names and personal email addresses to verify eligibility. What should the Treasurer do?
*   A) Send the email list immediately to ensure members can access the 15% discount.
*   B) Send the list, but only after charging the store a small administrative coordination fee.
*   C) Refuse to share the membership list. Instead, provide members with a physical or digital Union Discount Card that they can present at the store cash register to verify eligibility themselves.
*   D) Post the member list on the union bulletin board so the store can copy it.

**Correct Answer: C**
*Explanation*: Protecting member data is paramount. Unions must never share membership databases or personal contact info with commercial entities. Providing members with union-branded discount cards maintains security while delivering the affinity benefit.

### Question 3
Which of the following is the best example of "Community Unionism" in action?
*   A) Negotiating a contract clause that increases the Local President's cell phone allowance.
*   B) Partnering with local parent groups and community coalitions to campaign for increased public funding for school staffing, showing how safety in classrooms protects both workers and children.
*   C) Holding all Local Executive Board meetings at high-end, private downtown restaurants.
*   D) Refusing all contact with community groups to keep the local "purely workplace-focused."

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation*: Community Unionism connects workplace struggles to the broader public good. Campaigning alongside community partners for public safety and funding builds powerful, mutual solidarity that benefits both workers and the local community.

### Question 4
Your local wants to maximize ratification turnout. Best orientation practice tied to this module?
*   A) Explain that dues payers automatically vote — no card needed.
*   B) Sign membership cards during Day-1 orientation using `/guide/membership-signup` so new hires are eligible before the first vote.
*   C) Wait until a strike vote to introduce the union.
*   D) Email the membership list to a political campaign to remind members to vote.

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation*: Voting rights require a signed card; Day-1 signing converts new hires into eligible participants early — without sharing lists externally.

### Question 5
How does Module 14 differ from Module 6 (Building Collective Power)?
*   A) Module 14 replaces equity clauses and ADR with discount cards only.
*   B) Module 14 focuses on Day-1 welcome, affinity perks without list sharing, and community coalitions; Module 6 focuses on equity barriers, ADR, and bargaining equity language.
*   C) Module 6 is for stewards; Module 14 is only for employers.
*   D) They are identical — take only one.

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation*: Module 6 addresses structural equity and enforcement; Module 14 builds everyday belonging and public alliances — complementary, not interchangeable.

### Question 6
A community NGO asks for your membership email list to invite workers to a housing rally aligned with your bargaining themes. Best response?
*   A) Send the list — the cause is progressive.
*   B) Invite members through union channels you control; never release the database; co-host the public rally with the NGO.
*   C) Ignore the NGO — community work is off-mission.
*   D) Let the employer email members on the union's behalf.

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation*: Internal union communication preserves list privacy (Module 11); public coalition work happens in shared spaces, not via member data transfers.
